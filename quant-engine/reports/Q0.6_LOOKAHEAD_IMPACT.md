## Interpretation

**LOOK-AHEAD IMPACT: MATERIAL.**

Both directions of error appeared, which is itself important evidence
against a simpler "the bug always inflates performance" narrative: for
MACD, the bug *inflated* apparent performance (PF 1.76 → 1.15, a real
edge overstated as a much bigger one); for Bollinger, the bug
*understated* performance just as severely in the other direction (PF
0.26 → 0.99, a strategy that looked like a near-total loss was actually
close to breakeven once the bug was removed). This rules out a simple
"the bug always makes strategies look better than they are" story — it
made one strategy look much better and the other look much worse,
consistent with what a genuine timing/data-integrity defect would do
(scrambling *which* trades get taken and at what price, not
systematically biasing the outcome in one direction).

**Evidence this is a real, not incidental, effect:** post-fix,
`execution_mtf.py`'s numbers converge sharply toward `runner.py`'s
(which never had the bug) on both specs — see
`Q0.6_REVALIDATION_MATRIX.md`. This convergence, happening
simultaneously with the divergence from `execution_tick.py` (which
still has the bug), is strong, independent, real-data confirmation that
the fix — not some unrelated environmental change — caused this shift.

**Causality is not claimed beyond what's demonstrated.** This sprint
does not attempt to mathematically decompose the residual MACD gap
(PF 1.15 fixed vs. 1.07 `runner.py`) into "how much is spread" vs. "how
much is SL/TP resolution" — both are already-documented, legitimate
`execution_mtf.py` design choices (Q0.3), and isolating their individual
contributions would require additional controlled tests not run this
sprint.

## What this means for every prior report in this program

Q0.3 and Q0.4 both reported `execution_mtf.py`'s (and, by association,
`execution_tick.py`'s) dramatic outperformance over `runner.py` and
attributed it entirely to real-spread and fine-SL/TP-resolution
modeling. **That attribution was incomplete** — a material, now-measured
portion of it was the look-ahead artifact fixed this sprint. Q0.5's own
Bollinger checkpoint number (PF 0.19/0.26 depending on engine, −91.69%
return) was **not** evidence of a genuinely catastrophic strategy — it
was, in significant part, a data-integrity artifact. Once the bug is
fixed, Bollinger Mean Reversion is closer to breakeven (PF 0.99) than to
ruin. This does not retroactively validate any of those historical
numbers as correct — they remain accurately labeled as
`LEGACY-BACKTEST-EVIDENCE` from a since-fixed engine state — but it
substantially changes how much weight any of them should have carried
in the first place.
