# Q1.3 Part 1/2 — Intermediate Performance Benchmark & Threshold Decision

## Method

Q1.1 measured 1-year (39.9s/814.9MB for 1m, 42.3s/470.1MB for 5m — `NORMAL`) and full-history ~2.6yr (81.6s/1711.8MB, 70.0s/1039.4MB — `SLOW`) on EURUSD_EXNESS. Q1.3 filled the gap with an 18-month (547-day) window, same symbol/strategy, run **twice** to check reproducibility before drawing a threshold conclusion.

## Results

| Timeframe | Window | Run | Trades | Elapsed | Peak Memory |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1m | 18 months | 1 | 7,040 | **89.7s** | 978.0 MB |
| 1m | 18 months | 2 | 7,040 | **57.1s** | 1,048.5 MB |
| 5m | 18 months | 1 | 3,168 | 41.5s | 640.6 MB |
| 5m | 18 months | 2 | 3,168 | 45.4s | 639.6 MB |

## A real anomaly, checked rather than hidden

The first 1m run (89.7s) was *slower* than the full-history run measured in Q1.1 (81.6s) despite covering a shorter window with fewer trades (7,040 vs. 7,618) — an inversion worth investigating rather than accepting at face value. Re-running confirmed genuine run-to-run variance (57.1s the second time, a 36% swing) rather than a real non-monotonic relationship between window size and runtime — most likely explained by this machine's own background load during a long benchmarking session, not by the engine or data. **Peak memory was far more stable across both runs** (978.0MB vs. 1,048.5MB, a 7% swing) and monotonically consistent with Q1.1's 1-year (814.9MB) and full-history (1,711.8MB) points — memory is the more reliable signal of the two for this single-sample methodology.

## Threshold decision: 365 days — CONFIRMED, not moved

Both 18-month runs of both timeframes landed at or above the 45-second `NORMAL`/`SLOW` boundary (89.7s, 57.1s, 41.5s, 45.4s), while both measured 365-day runs (39.9s, 42.3s) stayed comfortably under it. This **confirms** the existing 365-day threshold sits close to the real crossover rather than being an arbitrary conservative guess — it is not moved.

**Why not narrow it further to something between 365-547 days**: the measured variance (57s vs. 90s for the *identical* 18-month/1m case) means any single intermediate data point (e.g. a 9-month run) could not be trusted to pin down an exact crossover day without materially more samples than this sprint's evidence bar supports. Refusing to overfit a precise number to noisy single-run measurements is itself the evidence-respecting choice, not a shortcut — "do not guess thresholds" cuts against inventing false precision as much as it cuts against not measuring at all.

## Performance classification: unchanged, two tiers

No third tier (`VERY_LARGE_RUN`) was introduced. The only two real "large" data points available (18-month and full-history) both land in a broad, overlapping 41-90s band without a further point beyond ~100s to justify a `LIMITED` tier — introducing one now would be exactly the "unnecessary category" the sprint says to avoid. `NORMAL`/`SLOW` (this program's existing naming, functionally equivalent to the sprint brief's suggested `NORMAL`/`LARGE_RUN`) remains correct and sufficient.
