# Q1.3 Part 3 — XAUUSD_EXNESS / XAUUSD_ZS_EXNESS Residual Coverage Spread

**Classification: ACCEPTABLE DATA CHARACTERISTIC (both symbols).**

## The residual under investigation

After Q1.2's `EXPECTED_SESSION_BREAK` fix resolved USOIL's spread (6.91→0.73 points), XAUUSD_EXNESS and XAUUSD_ZS_EXNESS retained a real, unexplained 1h-vs-4h coverage gap: 58.05% vs. 60.32% (2.27 points) and 43.64% vs. 45.88% (2.24 points) respectively.

## Method

Continued the Q1.2 investigation technique on real data (never modified). First checked whether the *absolute missing time* differs between 1h and 4h for XAUUSD_EXNESS: **279.67 days (1h) vs. 278.0 days (4h) — a difference of only 1.67 days**, nowhere near enough to explain a 2.27-point percentage spread on its own. This ruled out "4h is silently absorbing large real gaps that 1h correctly reports" — confirmed directly: every one of the 6 largest gaps (169.0d, 34.2d, 28.0d, 21.0d, 10.0d, 6.0d) has **identical start/end timestamps and duration at 1h, 4h, and 1d** — the big structural gaps are equally real and equally reported at every resolution, not smoothed away.

## Root cause — found and confirmed

The spread comes from the **denominator**, not the numerator. `coveragePct = (expected_trading_time - missing_time) / expected_trading_time`, and `expected_trading_time` excludes `EXPECTED_SESSION_BREAK` time (Q1.2). For XAUUSD_EXNESS:

| Timeframe | Coverage | Expected trading days | Session-break days excluded | Session-break count |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1h | 58.05% | 666.67 | **27.75** | **329** |
| 4h | 60.32% | 700.60 | **0.00** | **0** |

**A 1-4 hour daily session break is, by definition, smaller than a single 4-hour bar's own interval — it structurally cannot be detected as a gap at 4h resolution at all.** The underlying tick source still produces a valid 4h candle spanning that window (some real activity exists elsewhere in the 4 hours), so the same real, legitimate daily closure that 1h correctly excludes from its denominator is invisible to — and therefore implicitly counted as "available" by — the 4h series. XAUUSD_ZS_EXNESS shows the identical pattern (1h: 24.0 session-break days / 283 instances excluded; 4h: 0 / 0; absolute missing time nearly identical: 427.88d vs. 426.83d).

This is a **mechanical, structural consequence of gap-detection resolution**, not a data-quality defect, not a timestamp-alignment bug, and not a provider inconsistency: finer timeframes can see (and correctly exclude) real short closures that coarser timeframes cannot see at all.

## Explicit checks against the alternatives (all ruled out with evidence)

- **Legitimate session behavior**: yes — this *is* the explanation, confirmed above.
- **Provider-specific data**: no independent provider inconsistency found — the same tick source underlies every timeframe (Q1.0's audit already confirmed zero duplicate timestamps, zero OHLC violations).
- **Timeframe aggregation**: yes, in the specific sense of "a gap smaller than the target resolution's own bar interval cannot register" — this is inherent to any resampling scheme, not a defect in this program's import or gap-detection code.
- **Timestamp alignment**: no misalignment found — the 6 largest shared gaps have byte-identical boundaries across all three timeframes.
- **Missing data**: the missing data (169-day gap etc.) is real and identically reported everywhere — not the source of the residual spread.

## Does it materially affect Quant Lite backtesting?

**No.** `execution_mtf.py` trades on whatever real rows exist for the requested signal timeframe and 1-minute execution data — it never reads `coveragePct` and never behaves differently based on it. The coverage percentage is a disclosure metric only. A 1h backtest and a 4h backtest over the same nominal date range each correctly reflect their own resolution's real data; the fact that the *disclosed number* differs by ~2.3 points is honest and expected, not misleading — a 1h request already carries its own accurate, resolution-specific coverage figure regardless of what a 4h request over the same dates would show.

## Decision

No further action. Not force-matched, not smoothed, not classified as an issue requiring `market.db` or `execution_mtf.py` changes (neither was touched or needed). Documented as a permanent, expected characteristic of comparing coverage percentages across timeframes of different resolution for these two symbols (and, by the same structural logic, for any symbol/timeframe pair where `EXPECTED_SESSION_BREAK` gaps exist at a finer resolution than the one being compared against).
