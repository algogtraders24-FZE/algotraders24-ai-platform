## Canonical engine decision

**B. CANONICAL WITH REQUIRED FIXES.**

**Selected (target) engine: `execution_mtf.py`.**

### Technical reason

`execution_mtf.py`'s architecture is correct in every dimension except
one, and that one dimension has a specific, well-understood, narrowly
scoped defect — not a structural flaw requiring a redesign. Once fixed,
it would combine: real per-minute spread (not `runner.py`'s static
constant), genuine sub-bar SL/TP resolution (not `runner.py`'s
coarse whole-bar check), and — critically — it remains testable with
synthetic data and runs at a viable speed for library-scale rebuilds
(seconds, not the ~15-20 minutes per symbol-year `execution_tick.py`
requires). `execution_tick.py`'s tick-level fidelity is real and valuable
but not required to fix the specific defect found this sprint, and its
testability/performance limitations make it unsuitable as the *default*
canonical engine even after a fix, though it remains valuable as a
verification tool for individual candidates (consistent with
`QUANT_LITE_PRODUCT_BLUEPRINT.md`'s original framing of it as a
Quant-Pro-tier differentiator).

### Evidence

- Real-data trade-ledger reconciliation (MACD Crossover, 2024,
  `XAUUSD_EXNESS`): `execution_mtf.py`'s trade #2 exits ~18 hours before
  `runner.py`'s identically-parameterized trade, causing a downstream
  entry/direction divergence (BUY vs. SELL continuation) — root-caused
  to `execution_mtf.py:114`'s bar-closed check firing at a left-labeled
  bar's *start* rather than its close.
- Independent, controlled synthetic confirmation
  (`scripts/q05_synthetic_tests.py`, TEST F/G): `execution_mtf.py`
  entered a position at minute 0 of a deliberately-constructed jump bar,
  at the pre-jump price — a result impossible without acting on data
  before it was genuinely available.
- Same defect confirmed at the identical source-code pattern in
  `execution_tick.py:105`.
- Determinism confirmed for all three engines (this sprint) — the bug
  is consistent and reproducible, not a source of nondeterminism, which
  is exactly why it was traceable to a specific, fixable root cause
  rather than an unexplainable anomaly.

### Known limitations, even after the required fix

- Still no slippage model, no functioning commission model, no session
  filter, no broker minimum-stop-distance check (shared by all three
  engines, not specific to `execution_mtf.py`).
- The exact magnitude of how much of Q0.3/Q0.4's reported PF
  improvement (`runner.py` → `execution_mtf.py`) was genuine modeling
  improvement versus this bug's artifact is **not quantified** — that
  requires re-running the comparison with the fix applied, which is a
  Q0.6 task, not resolved here.

### Required fixes (not implemented this sprint)

1. Correct the signal-bar-closed check in `execution_mtf.py` (and, if
   `execution_tick.py` is retained for verification use, there too) to
   account for left-labeled candle timestamps — a bar timestamped `T`
   at timeframe `Δ` is not closed until `T + Δ`, not at `T` itself.
2. Re-run the exact MACD/Bollinger 2024 comparison in this report after
   the fix, to (a) confirm the fix closes the gap with `runner.py`'s
   trade-3-onward divergence, and (b) quantify how much of the
   previously reported PF improvement survives the fix.
3. Only after (2) should `execution_mtf.py` be treated as genuinely
   canonical rather than "the intended target, pending verification."

### Why the other engines were not selected

- **`runner.py`** is not selected as canonical, despite having no
  confirmed correctness bug, because it is a strictly weaker
  approximation of real execution on every dimension `execution_mtf.py`
  gets right once fixed (spread realism, SL/TP resolution). Recommending
  it as the long-term canonical engine would mean deliberately keeping
  the coarser model to avoid a fixable bug, rather than fixing the bug.
  It remains valuable and is not retired — see classification below.
- **`execution_tick.py`** is not selected because its structural
  inability to accept synthetic test data and its ~15-20-minute-per-run
  cost make it unsuitable as the engine a Quant Lite product would run
  by default, independent of the shared bug. Its real-tick fidelity is
  a genuine strength reserved for spot-verification, not default use.

## Legacy engine classification

| Engine | Classification | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| `runner.py` | **KEEP / RESEARCH-REFERENCE** | Correct signal timing, simplest, fully synthetic-testable; not the canonical target due to weaker fill-price realism, but valuable as a cross-check and as the historical `demo.py`-reproduction reference |
| `execution_mtf.py` | **FIX — canonical-in-waiting** | Best architecture, one confirmed and well-scoped defect; becomes the canonical Quant Lite engine once fixed and re-verified |
| `execution_tick.py` | **KEEP / RESEARCH-ONLY** | Same defect as `execution_mtf.py`, plus real testability and performance limitations that keep it out of the default path even post-fix; retained for individual-candidate spot-verification, consistent with its original positioning in `QUANT_LITE_PRODUCT_BLUEPRINT.md` §6 |

No engine is retired. No engine was modified.

## Product entry-point check (Step 14)

Repository-wide search confirms: **no product-facing entry point for
`quant_lite_risk_config()` exists yet.** Every consumer found is a
demo/verification script (`demo.py`, `scripts/test_quant_lite_baseline.py`,
`scripts/q05_cross_engine_test.py`, etc.) — none is a named,
product-shaped API or CLI surface.

**Result: C. No product-facing entry point exists yet.**

Documented, not implemented this sprint per the sprint's explicit
preference. When a real entry point is eventually built, it should:
(a) import `execution_mtf.py` **only after** the required fix above
lands and is verified, not before, and (b) use
`quant_lite_risk_config()` exactly as `scripts/test_quant_lite_baseline.py`
and `scripts/q05_cross_engine_test.py` already demonstrate.

`build_one_library.py` calls `run_spec_backtest()` (`runner.py`), which
does not have this defect. This is a small, incidental piece of good
news: the library's `LEGACY-BACKTEST-EVIDENCE` numbers, while still
correctly classified as not-yet-validated per Q0.3/Q0.4, are at least
not additionally compromised by the specific bug found this sprint.
