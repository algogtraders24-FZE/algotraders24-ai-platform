## Trade-ledger reconciliation, MACD Crossover

First two trades (Jan 2, 2024) agree across all three engines exactly
in PnL (entry-price differences are the already-documented,
already-understood static-vs-real spread difference):

| Trade | `runner.py` | `execution_mtf.py` | `execution_tick.py` |
|---|---|---|---|
| #1 | entry 2024-01-02 00:00, BUY @ 2065.68, TP, PnL +152.86 | entry 2024-01-02 00:00, BUY @ 2064.55, TP, PnL +152.86 | entry 2024-01-02 00:00:00.064, BUY @ 2063.91, TP, PnL +152.86 |
| #2 | entry 2024-01-02 12:00, SELL @ 2067.82, **exit 2024-01-03 09:00**, TP, PnL +146.52 | entry 2024-01-02 12:00, SELL @ 2069.17, **exit 2024-01-02 15:37**, TP, PnL +146.52 | entry 2024-01-02 12:00:00.593, SELL @ 2069.22, **exit 2024-01-02 15:37:27**, TP, PnL +146.52 |

**First material divergence: trade #2's exit time.** `runner.py` reports
the TP hit at `2024-01-03 09:00` — a full **18 hours** after
`execution_mtf.py`/`execution_tick.py` both report it (`2024-01-02
15:37`), even though all three used the identical TP distance and PnL
came out identical. This is not a granularity-precision difference of
the kind already documented in Q0.3 (spread, sub-bar SL/TP resolution)
— it propagates: because `runner.py`'s position stays open ~18 hours
longer, `runner.py`'s trade #3 is a **different trade entirely** —
a SELL continuation entered at `2024-01-03 09:00` — while
`execution_mtf.py`/`execution_tick.py`'s trade #3 is a **BUY**, entered
at `2024-01-03 04:00`, that `runner.py` never takes at all (its
position was still artificially open).

## Causal investigation — this is the confirmed bug

Pulled the real `XAUUSD_EXNESS` 1h candles for the window in question
directly from `market.db`:

```
2024-01-02T12:00:00  open=2069.28  high=2069.60  low=2065.11  close=2067.97
2024-01-02T13:00:00  open=2067.93  high=2071.13  low=2064.90  close=2066.35
2024-01-02T14:00:00  open=2066.41  high=2070.47  low=2062.23  close=2070.42
2024-01-02T15:00:00  open=2070.47  high=2070.79  low=2056.20  close=2059.41
```

The SELL TP level (≈2057) is touched by the **15:00 bar's own low
(2056.20)** — a real, legitimate touch within that hour. `runner.py`
checks each bar's own high/low every iteration, so it should have
detected this by its 15:00 iteration, not 18 hours later. It didn't.
Root cause, confirmed at the source level (identical in both
`execution_mtf.py:114` and `execution_tick.py:105`):

```python
while sig_ptr < len(sig_rows) and sig_ts[sig_ptr] <= ts_i:
    cur_sig = sig_rows[sig_ptr]
    ...
```

`sig_ts` is `candles.ts`, which `scripts/import_exness.py` populates
with **left-labeled** bins (`resample(rule, label="left", closed="left")`
— confirmed in that file) — meaning a candle timestamped `12:00`
represents the bar spanning `[12:00, 13:00)`, and is not actually
*closed* until `13:00`. But the condition above treats that bar as
closed the instant an execution-timeframe timestamp reaches `>= 12:00`
— i.e., **at the bar's own start**, a full bar-period early.

**This is why `execution_mtf.py`'s entry #2 fires with `entry_price ≈
2069.17`, essentially the 12:00 bar's own *open* (2069.28), not its
close (2067.97).** Confirmed numerically for trade #1 too:
`execution_tick.py`'s entry price (2063.91) sits within $0.12 of the
00:00 bar's real open (2063.79) — nowhere near its close (2065.53).

**Independent, controlled confirmation** (not just real-data
inference): a synthetic scenario (`scripts/q05_synthetic_tests.py`,
TEST F/G) built a flat 55-bar warmup followed by a single clean price
jump on bar 55, with 1-minute sub-bars **deliberately held at the OLD
price for the entire hour except the final minute**. `execution_mtf.py`
entered at **minute 0** of that bar, at the **old, pre-jump price**
(2000.05) — a result only possible if it acted on the bar before its
real close (2050, only knowable at minute 59) was actually known.
`runner.py` did not enter in this specific synthetic case (a secondary,
unresolved question — see Open Items below) — but the synthetic proof
of `execution_mtf.py`'s early-fire behavior stands independent of that.

**This is genuine look-ahead bias, not merely an execution-timing
approximation.** The MACD cross-condition driving this entry needs the
signal bar's own close to be valid; both `execution_mtf.py` and
`execution_tick.py` act on it up to one full signal-bar-period before
that close is real. It is present in both of this session's newer
engines and **absent from `runner.py`**, which — despite being the
"coarse, legacy" engine per every prior report in this program — turns
out to have the more causally correct signal-timing model.

## Implication for every prior report in this program

Q0.3 and Q0.4 both observed and reported the dramatic swing from
`runner.py`'s numbers to `execution_mtf.py`/`execution_tick.py`'s (e.g.
Q0.3: MACD PF 0.95 → 2.05 → 2.44) and attributed it to real-spread
modeling and finer SL/TP resolution — both real, both previously
verified, **both still true**. This sprint's finding does not overturn
those — it adds a **third, previously unknown contributor** that was
never isolated: some portion of `execution_mtf.py`/`execution_tick.py`'s
apparent outperformance is not superior modeling, it's premature entries
using data that would not have been available in real time. **The exact
split between "better modeling" and "look-ahead artifact" is not
quantified by this sprint** — that would require re-running the
comparison with the bug fixed, which is out of scope (no engine
modification permitted this sprint). This is recorded as an open item
for Q0.6, not resolved here.
