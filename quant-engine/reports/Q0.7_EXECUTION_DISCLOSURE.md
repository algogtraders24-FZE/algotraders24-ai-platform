# Q0.7 — Quant Lite Execution Disclosure

The exact disclosure that must appear alongside every Quant Lite
backtest result. Written to be shown to a user, not just read internally
— plain language, no jargon left unexplained, nothing claimed that isn't
technically true of the canonical `execution_mtf.py` engine as verified
through Q0.3–Q0.6.

---

## Quant Lite Execution Model — What This Backtest Does and Does Not Do

**Signal timing.** Your strategy's entry/exit conditions are checked
once per completed bar on your chosen timeframe (e.g. once per hour for
a 1-hour strategy). A signal is only acted on after that bar has
genuinely finished forming — never before.

**Order fills.** Once a signal fires, this simulator fills the order
using real historical minute-by-minute price data, at the real spread
that existed in the market at that moment — not an average, not a
guess, and not a rounded estimate.

**Spread.** Modeled using real, historical bid/ask spread data,
averaged per minute. Spread cost is applied to every trade, exactly as
a real broker would apply it.

**Slippage.** **Not modeled.** Every simulated order fills at exactly
the intended price. In live or demo trading, fast-moving markets can
cause your actual fill price to differ from what this backtest assumes.

**Commission.** **Not modeled.** This backtest does not deduct any
per-trade commission. If your broker charges commission, your real
results would be worse than what this backtest shows.

**Stop-loss and take-profit.** Modeled with minute-level precision —
this simulator checks every minute of real price movement to determine
whether your stop or target was hit, not just the coarse high/low of
the full signal bar.

**Breakeven stops, trailing stops, and partial position closing.**
**Not modeled in Quant Lite.** Your position is opened with a
stop-loss and take-profit that never move and never close early. This
is a deliberate simplification: it exactly matches the position
management (or lack of it) in the MQL5, MQL4, and TradingView Pine
Script code this same strategy specification would generate — so a
Quant Lite backtest result describes the same trading behavior the
exported code actually implements. (Advanced position management is a
Quant Pro feature — see the upgrade page.)

**Tick-level execution.** **Not modeled in Quant Lite.** This backtest
resolves order fills at 1-minute resolution, not by replaying every
individual real market tick. On the rare occasion your stop-loss and
take-profit are both crossed within the same minute, this backtest
conservatively assumes the stop-loss was hit first. (Tick-level replay
is a Quant Pro feature.)

**Historical data.** Backtests run against real historical price data
with defined coverage — not synthetic or estimated data — but that
coverage is limited to specific date ranges per symbol, and different
symbols in this system come from different original data sources. The
exact data source and date range used are always shown with your
results.

**This backtest is not a prediction.** A backtest run entirely on
historical data. It shows how a strategy *would have* performed under
the stated assumptions above, on the data available. It is not a
forecast, and it is not a guarantee of how the strategy will perform on
new data or in live trading.

**Deterministic.** Running the exact same strategy against the exact
same data and settings will always produce the exact same result — this
backtest contains no randomness of any kind.

## Explicitly prohibited phrasing (per instruction)

The Quant Lite product must never say, imply, or design UI copy around:

- "Accurate future performance" — a backtest describes the past under
  stated assumptions, never the future.
- "Guaranteed results" — nothing in this system guarantees any outcome.
- "Real market execution" — real historical *price* data, yes; real
  *order execution* (slippage, commission, broker-specific fills,
  margin/stop-out behavior), no. The distinction must stay visible.

## Language actually supported by the engine

Acceptable, accurate phrasing: "backtested against real historical
price and spread data," "minute-level stop-loss/take-profit precision,"
"fully deterministic — the same inputs always produce the same result,"
"does not model slippage or commission," "matches the position
management of the exported strategy code."
