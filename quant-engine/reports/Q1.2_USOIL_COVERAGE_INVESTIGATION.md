# Q1.2 Part 1 — USOIL_EXNESS Cross-Timeframe Coverage Investigation

**Classification: RESOLVED.**

## The finding under investigation

Q1.1 measured USOIL_EXNESS's whole-range coverage at 89.88% (1h) vs. 96.71-96.79% (4h/1d) — a 6.91-point spread, the largest of any symbol audited.

## Method

Built `quant-engine/scripts/q12_usoil_investigation.py` (read-only) to test a specific hypothesis directly against real data, rather than guessing: **a coarser-timeframe bar only needs one real tick anywhere in its own window to exist, so a genuine short outage visible in the 1h series can be "absorbed" (invisible) once resampled to 4h/1d.** For every real (non-closure) 1h gap, the script checks whether a 4h/1d bar exists starting inside that gap's window, and spot-checks that the covering bar has real, non-flat OHLC (i.e. genuine ticks, not a carried-forward placeholder).

## Result 1 — the absorption effect is real and measured

370 of USOIL_EXNESS's 541 real 1h gaps (68%) have a 4h bar starting inside them — accounting for 50.46 of the 68.58 total missing-days (73.6%). Every spot-checked covering 4h bar had genuine, distinct OHLC values (`flat=False`) — confirmed via direct SQL, not inferred. This is **legitimate aggregation behavior**: the underlying market genuinely had some activity within a given 4-hour window even when a specific 1-hour sub-window inside it did not. The same effect was confirmed on two other symbols for comparison (Part 2): XAUUSD_EXNESS absorbs 95.4% of its missing 1h-days at 4h; EURUSD_EXNESS absorbs 86.7%.

## Result 2 — why USOIL specifically shows a *larger* spread than EURUSD

The remaining, non-absorbed 1h gaps (real holes visible at every timeframe) split into two evidenced categories:

1. **A genuine daily rollover pattern.** 510 of USOIL_EXNESS's 512 short (1-4 hour) gaps start at 20:00-22:00 UTC, spanning 509 distinct calendar days — roughly 74% of all eligible trading days in the dataset. XAUUSD_EXNESS shows the same pattern (322+ distinct days). **EURUSD_EXNESS, GBPUSD_EXNESS, and BTCUSD_EXNESS show only 6-8 such gaps total** — not a systematic pattern. This is a real, provider-side daily settlement/rollover window specific to these commodity/metal instruments, not a data defect — evidenced by sheer frequency and time-of-day consistency, not assumed. Reclassified as `EXPECTED_SESSION_BREAK` (Q1.2 rule addition, `AUDIT_RULE_VERSION` bumped to `q1.2-gap-rules-v2` — see `Q1.2_MARKET_CLOSURE_POLICY.md`).
2. **Calendar-holiday-shaped gaps** (Dec 24-25, Dec 31-Jan 1, recurring across 2024/2025/2026) that fall on weekdays other than the modeled Thu/Fri/Sat weekly-closure start days, so were not previously absorbed into `EXPECTED_MARKET_CLOSURE`. Documented, not reclassified this sprint (see `Q1.2_MARKET_CLOSURE_POLICY.md` for why a full calendar was not built).

**Answer to "why does USOIL specifically show a bigger spread than EURUSD?"**: because USOIL genuinely has far more real, short, provider-side daily gaps to be absorbed by resampling than EURUSD does — the discrepancy is a real reflection of a real difference in how these two instrument classes are quoted by this data provider, not a bug in either timeframe's data.

## Result 3 — confirmed NOT explained by

- **Duplicate/misaligned timestamps**: zero found in either series (Q1.0's original OHLC/duplicate audit, re-confirmed).
- **A resampling defect in this program's own tooling**: the gap registry queries `market.db`'s already-stored 1h/4h/1d rows directly — it does not itself resample anything; whatever resampling produced those rows happened upstream, during import (`quant-engine/scripts/import_exness.py`, untouched this sprint).
- **A bug in the Q1.1 coverage-ratio formula**: confirmed correct on its own terms (duration-based, closure-excluded) — the discrepancy is inherent to what "coverage" means differently at different resolutions when short gaps exist, not a computation error.

## What was NOT done

Per instruction: no data was changed, no timeframe was forced to agree with another, no discrepancy was smoothed or averaged away. Each timeframe is still audited fully independently and will continue to show different coverage numbers — that is now understood and documented as expected behavior, not fixed to look uniform.
