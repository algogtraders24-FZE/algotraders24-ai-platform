# Q0.2 Market Structure Contracts

Type-only contracts (`src/domain/market-structure.ts`) for price-action/market-structure concepts, per Q0.2.17. **No detection algorithm is implemented in Q0.2** — these are precise, non-vague definitions of the shape each concept's output must eventually take, so a future detector (and a future strategy referencing its output) has a stable target to build against. This naming set deliberately mirrors terminology already used in this repo's MQL5 EA research (`ea-research/G01_LiquiditySweep_MSS_FVG/`) for consistency — this document does not read, import, or depend on that EA's code in any way; it is a fresh, independent type design.

## Shared shape

Every concept extends `MarketStructureEventBase`:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `instrument`, `timeframe` | which market/timeframe this was detected on |
| `detectedAt` | timestamp of the bar that produced this detection (may lag the event itself — see `barTimestamp` on `SwingPoint`) |
| `detectionState` | `"forming"` (not yet confirmed), `"confirmed"` (settled), `"invalidated"` (later price action disproved it) |
| `repaintingPolicy` | `"final"` (can never change once detected), `"provisional-may-repaint"` (could still be revised/withdrawn by later bars), `"provisional-confirmed-on-close"` (becomes final once its defining bar closes) |

The `detectionState`/`repaintingPolicy` pair exists because market-structure concepts are notorious for being computed differently by "confirmed on close" vs. "live/repainting" implementations — this contract forces every future detector to declare which behavior it has, rather than leaving it implicit and a frequent source of backtest-vs-live divergence.

## SwingPoint

**Definition**: A local price extreme — a bar whose high (for a swing high) or low (for a swing low) is more extreme than some number of bars on both sides (the exact lookback is a detector parameter, not fixed by this contract).
**Input**: a window of `OHLCVBar`s.
**Output**: `{ direction: "high" | "low", price, barTimestamp }` — `barTimestamp` is the actual swing bar's timestamp, which may differ from `detectedAt` since confirming a swing requires observing bars *after* it.
**Confidence/quality**: not applicable — a swing point is binary (it either is or isn't a local extreme under the detector's lookback rule).
**Repainting**: typically `"provisional-confirmed-on-close"` — a swing at bar N is provisional until enough subsequent bars confirm no more extreme point exists within the lookback window.

## BreakOfStructure (BOS)

**Definition**: Price closes beyond a prior swing point **in the direction of the existing trend**, confirming trend continuation.
**Input**: a `SwingPoint` (the level broken) plus the breaking bar.
**Output**: `{ direction: "bullish" | "bearish", brokenLevel, referenceSwing }`.
**Confidence/quality**: not applicable in this base contract.
**Repainting**: `"final"` once the closing bar that broke the level is itself final — a BOS is a closed-bar event, not typically revised.

## ChangeOfCharacter (CHoCH)

**Definition**: Price closes beyond a prior swing point **against the existing trend**, signaling a potential trend change (as distinct from BOS, which confirms the existing trend).
**Input/Output**: same shape as `BreakOfStructure`.
**Repainting**: same as BOS — `"final"` on bar close.

## MarketStructureShift (MSS)

**Definition**: A CHoCH that a detector additionally judges structurally significant enough to treat as an actual shift in market bias (the distinction between "a CHoCH happened" and "the market structure has shifted" is a detector-specific judgment call, hence the optional `confidence`).
**Input**: same as CHoCH.
**Output**: `{ direction, brokenLevel, referenceSwing, confidence? }` — `confidence` is `[0,1]`, optional because not every detector will produce one.
**Repainting**: `"final"` once its defining CHoCH is final; a detector MAY additionally revise its `confidence` value on a `"provisional-may-repaint"` basis before that — this contract permits but does not mandate that behavior.

## LiquidityLevel

**Definition**: A price level where resting orders (stops, pending orders) are inferred to cluster — equal highs/lows, session highs/lows, or an untested swing level.
**Output**: `{ levelKind: "equal-highs" | "equal-lows" | "session-high" | "session-low" | "swing-level", price }`.
**Repainting**: `"provisional-may-repaint"` — a level's classification (e.g. whether two highs count as "equal") often depends on a tolerance parameter that later price action can retroactively affect the interpretation of.

## LiquiditySweep

**Definition**: Price briefly trades through a `LiquidityLevel` and then closes back on the other side (`reversed: true`) or continues through it as a genuine breakout (`reversed: false`).
**Input**: the `LiquidityLevel` and the sweeping bar(s).
**Output**: `{ sweptLevel, sweepPrice, reversed }`.
**Repainting**: `"provisional-confirmed-on-close"` — whether it's a genuine reversal vs. a breakout typically can't be judged until at least the sweeping bar closes.

## FairValueGap (FVG)

**Definition**: A three-bar imbalance where bar 1's high/low does not overlap with bar 3's low/high, leaving a "gap" in traded price.
**Output**: `{ direction: "bullish" | "bearish", gapHigh, gapLow, filledFraction? }` — `filledFraction` (`[0,1]`) tracks how much of the gap has since been traded through, since FVGs are commonly treated as "partially mitigated" rather than binary filled/unfilled.
**Repainting**: `"final"` for the gap's existence and bounds (fixed once bar 3 closes) but `filledFraction` is inherently `"provisional-may-repaint"` since it updates every time price revisits the gap.

## OrderBlock

**Definition**: The last opposing-direction candle before a displacement move, interpreted as the origin of institutional order flow.
**Output**: `{ direction: "bullish" | "bearish", high, low, mitigated }` — `mitigated` tracks whether price has since returned to and traded through the block.
**Repainting**: `"provisional-may-repaint"` — different detectors define "the last opposing candle" differently depending on how much subsequent structure they require before confirming a displacement occurred, so an order block's boundaries can shift as more bars arrive.

## Displacement

**Definition**: An unusually large, fast price move (relative to recent volatility), often used to validate that a preceding order block or FVG is significant rather than noise.
**Output**: `{ direction, magnitude, barCount }` — `magnitude` is the price distance covered, `barCount` the number of bars over which it occurred.
**Repainting**: `"final"` once the bars comprising the move have closed.

## What is explicitly deferred

No detection algorithm, no threshold/tolerance defaults, no lookback-window defaults, and no confidence-scoring formula are specified here — those are future-detector implementation choices. This document fixes only the *shape* every future detector must produce, so strategies and tests written against these types today will not need to change when a real detector is eventually built.
