# Q0.4 Cross-Platform Research

Q0.4.1: deep research across MT4, MT5, TradingView/Pine, NinjaTrader, cTrader, QuantConnect/LEAN, and Backtrader — their backtesting/execution semantics specifically, not general usage. Findings below are drawn from live research against each platform's official documentation and (for LEAN) its open-source code, conducted for this sprint; sources are cited inline. Where a claim could not be independently verified, it is flagged explicitly rather than presented as fact. TradingView/Pine's full treatment lives in `Q0.4_PINE_SEMANTICS.md` (Q0.4.2's dedicated first-class research target) — only a summary appears here for cross-platform comparability.

---

## MetaTrader 4 (MQL4)

**DATA MODEL**: No real tick history in the Strategy Tester — MT4 generates its testing price stream entirely from OHLC bar data, using the nearest available lower timeframe as the interpolation source. Tick volume only (count of price changes), no real exchange volume — MT4 targets OTC/FX markets. No Depth-of-Market simulation in the tester. Historical data quality is a widely cited limitation across trading-education sources: bar-derived tick simulation "can lead to inaccuracies, especially in fast-moving strategies like scalping."

**STRATEGY MODEL**: `OnTick()` fires on every incoming quote for the EA's chart symbol (modern MQL4, post-build-600; legacy `start()` in older syntax). Not used by indicators/scripts (`OnCalculate()`/legacy `start()` instead). State persists via global/static variables for the test's duration. No formally-documented push-based trade-event handler was found for MQL4 (contrast with MQL5's `OnTrade()`, below) — MQL4's trade-state story is closer to "check manually on next tick."

**ORDER MODEL**: Flat, ticket-based — `OP_BUY`/`OP_SELL` (market), `OP_BUYLIMIT`/`OP_SELLLIMIT`, `OP_BUYSTOP`/`OP_SELLSTOP` (pending); **no stop-limit type**. Every order/position is one integer ticket; `OrderSelect()` must precede any accessor. No separate order/deal/position objects — a "position" IS a ticket. Pending-order expiry is a fixed `datetime` or GTC (0), broker-enforced.

**EXECUTION MODEL**: Exactly three Strategy Tester modes (official MQL5 article "Strategy Tester: Modes of Modeling during Testing"): **Every Tick** (builds control points from the smallest available sub-timeframe's OHLC, interpolated via "predefined templates" — the article's own caveat: pointless "if there are no smaller timeframes fully covering the timeframe under test"); **Control Points** (a cruder method, ~12-point fractal interpolation off the next-lower timeframe, filled with template waves when that data is missing — the article calls these results "estimated only, not final"); **Open Prices Only** (O=H=L=C, Volume=1, evaluated once per bar — for bar-close-only strategies). Spread is historically fixed in tester options; no native floating-spread/slippage/latency simulation.

**POSITION MODEL**: **Always hedging-like, by construction** — there is no netting mode; every order is its own independently-tracked ticket, so opposite-direction tickets on the same symbol simply coexist rather than netting against each other. Pyramiding/scale-in is implicit (every `OrderSend()` is a new, independent ticket) — there is no position-size aggregation unless the EA does it itself.

**ACCOUNT MODEL**: Standard balance/equity/margin/leverage/margin-call/stop-out fields, configurable in tester account settings; MT4-specific formula documentation distinct from MT5's was not independently located.

**TIME MODEL**: `TimeCurrent()`/`TimeLocal()` track the replayed quote stream, not wall-clock. `TimeGMT()` in the tester does **not** return true GMT — it reflects broker server time, and brokers are free to choose inconsistent DST conventions (some mix an EU timezone with US DST rules). The tester inherits whatever convention the underlying history data was stored with; it does not independently correct for DST.

**BACKTEST MODEL**: Purely bar-derived (no real tick ingestion). Optimization is brute-force/exhaustive only — no evidence found of a native genetic algorithm in MT4 specifically (that arrived with MT5). **No native Walk-Forward Optimization and no native Monte Carlo** — explicitly confirmed via community sourcing; third-party libraries (WalkForwardOptimizer, DATFRA, ErgodicLabs) exist to fill this gap externally.

## MetaTrader 5 (MQL5)

**DATA MODEL**: **Five** tester modes (official docs): Every Tick, **Every tick based on real ticks**, 1-Minute OHLC, Open Prices Only, Math Calculations (no price simulation — pure parameter optimization). "Every tick based on real ticks" is a verified, genuine feature — it uses actual historical tick data accumulated by the broker's trade server, introduced in build 1325 (April 22, 2016), falling back to generated ticks only where real tick history has gaps. The **tick-generation algorithm** for generated ticks (published as a dedicated MQL5 article, "The Algorithm of Ticks' Generation...") caps reference points per bar at the bar's tick volume (max 11), using a 3-5-3-point distribution scheme across the bar's shadows/body with formulaic fractional positioning and linear interpolation between points — i.e. even MT5's "generated" ticks are a documented, non-arbitrary algorithm, not a black box. Spread within generated ticks is **fixed per 1-minute bar** (Ask = Bid + that minute's fixed spread); real-tick mode allows genuine intra-minute spread variation. Real exchange volume exists only for centralized/exchange markets — Forex/OTC instruments get tick volume only. Depth of Market is **not available in the tester** even though it exists live.

**STRATEGY MODEL**: Three formally documented events — `OnTick()` (new quote, fires regardless of AutoTrading state), `OnTimer()` (explicit fixed-interval timer via `EventSetTimer()`/`EventKillTimer()`), `OnTrade()` (fires on order/position/deal list changes — the push-based trade-event handler MQL4 lacks). The terminal **coalesces** same-type events — a queued-or-processing `OnTick`/`OnTimer` is not re-queued, so handlers can be skipped under load. Indicators use `OnCalculate()` with a `prev_calculated` parameter enabling incremental recomputation; the terminal resets it to 0 (forcing full recompute) whenever the underlying series changes retroactively.

**ORDER MODEL**: Formal **Order → Deal → Position** separation — Order is the instruction, Deal is the executed record (one order can produce multiple deals via partial fills), Position is the net long/short exposure per symbol (under netting accounting specifically — one net position per instrument-per-account in that mode). Order types include a genuine **stop-limit** (`ORDER_TYPE_BUY_STOP_LIMIT`/`SELL_STOP_LIMIT`, absent from MT4) — once triggered, it places a pending limit rather than going to market. Four expiry modes: `ORDER_TIME_GTC`, `ORDER_TIME_DAY`, `ORDER_TIME_SPECIFIED`, `ORDER_TIME_SPECIFIED_DAY`, constrained per-symbol by broker-set properties. A "Return" fill policy leaves a partial fill's remainder still working rather than cancelling it.

**EXECUTION MODEL**: Explicit three-mode **execution delay** setting: No Delay (idealized), Random Delay (~90% chance of 0-8s, ~10% chance of 9-18s — a documented, specific distribution, not vague "some randomness"), Fixed Delay (user-set constant, informed by measured ping). Commission modeling is richer than MT4's: basis (money/percent/points) × timing (per-trade/daily/monthly) × directionality (open-only/close-only/both) — all independently configurable.

**POSITION MODEL**: **The single most important MT4-vs-MT5 distinction, verified precisely.** MT5 introduced a broker-configured, account-level `ENUM_ACCOUNT_MARGIN_MODE`: `ACCOUNT_MARGIN_MODE_RETAIL_NETTING` (one net position per symbol — MT5's original design), `ACCOUNT_MARGIN_MODE_RETAIL_HEDGING` (added in build 1325 specifically to let multiple, including opposite-direction, positions coexist per symbol — deliberately built to feel familiar to MT4 users), and `ACCOUNT_MARGIN_MODE_EXCHANGE` (for genuine exchange markets). **This mode is set by the broker at account creation, not toggleable per-EA or per-trade** — an EA can only query it. Under netting, pyramiding/scale-in orders merge into one volume-weighted-average position; under hedging, MT5 behaves like MT4's ticket model. MT4 has exactly one behavior; MT5's behavior is a binary configuration that changes the entire order/position semantics of any EA running on it.

**ACCOUNT MODEL**: `ACCOUNT_MARGIN_CALL_LEVEL` (no-new-positions threshold) and `ACCOUNT_MARGIN_STOPOUT_LEVEL` (forced-closure threshold, largest losing position first, per documented convention) — both queryable and tester-configurable.

**TIME MODEL**: Both platforms are broker-server-time driven, not tied to the broker's real geographic timezone (commonly aligned to a "5pm New York close" FX convention). MT5-specific `TimeGMT()`/tester-timezone behavior parity with MT4 was **not independently confirmed** via an MT5-specific doc page in this research pass — treat MT4's findings as likely-representative but unconfirmed for MT5 specifically.

**BACKTEST MODEL**: Spans the full spectrum from pure bar-only through generated-tick to genuine tick replay — strictly richer than MT4's three bar-derived-only modes. Two optimization engines: **Slow Complete** (exhaustive, can take "several years" for large spaces) and **Fast Genetic** (evolutionary, automatically forced on above 100 million combinations). **No native Walk-Forward Optimization and no native Monte Carlo** in either platform — both rely on third-party tools; no official MetaQuotes "deep backtesting" branded concept was found for either.

**Cross-cutting limitations (both platforms, from community/education sourcing)**: fidelity is capped by whatever history the specific broker's server actually has (commonly much better for recent history than older); non-real-tick modes use a spread fixed per bar, missing news-driven spread widening unless real tick data supplies it; neither tester fully mimics news spikes, thin liquidity, or queue position; same-bar SL/TP ambiguity is explicitly cited in community discussion as producing materially different results across the different modeling modes for the identical EA/parameters; slippage is often left unmodeled by default, producing "overly optimistic" results.

## QuantConnect / LEAN

**DATA MODEL**: Five resolutions (`Tick`/`Second`/`Minute`/`Hour`/`Daily`) via subscription; **Consolidators** derive coarser bars from a finer subscription (never the reverse — you cannot synthesize finer data from coarser). Two bar types: `TradeBar` (OHLCV from trades) and `QuoteBar` (bid/ask-native, with `Open`/`High`/`Low`/`Close` computed as the mean of the corresponding Bid/Ask values, falling back to whichever side has data). The `Slice` object is LEAN's atomic, TIME-SYNCHRONIZED data packet delivered to `OnData` — it carries every subscribed security's current data (regardless of each one's native resolution) aligned to the same instant, which is the mechanism underpinning LEAN's deterministic multi-resolution/multi-asset processing. **Survivorship-bias-free** US equity data (1998-present) is a genuine, verified, explicitly-marketed feature: a "Security Master" tracks historical listing/delisting/ticker-mapping/corporate-actions, includes since-delisted companies for the periods they were actually tradable, and auto-liquidates positions at actual delisting rather than silently excluding failed companies from history.

**STRATEGY MODEL**: `Initialize()` (once) + `OnData(Slice)` (every synchronized timeslice with new data) as the core loop, plus `Schedule.On(DateRules, TimeRules, callback)` for wall-clock-triggered logic independent of data arrival — LEAN specifically back-fills scheduled events that would have been missed due to a data gap, rather than silently skipping them. The **Algorithm Framework** is a distinctive, verified design: five pluggable, independently-swappable modules chained together — Universe Selection → Alpha Model (emits `Insight` objects: direction/magnitude/confidence, deliberately decoupled from sizing) → Portfolio Construction Model (converts Insights to `PortfolioTarget`s) → Risk Management Model (can override/adjust targets) → Execution Model (converts targets to actual orders, with pluggable execution algorithms). This is a strong precedent for AT24's own Decision → Risk Evaluation → Order Engine separation (Q0.3/`Q0.4_BACKTEST_ARCHITECTURE.md`) — LEAN independently arrived at splitting "what to believe" from "how much to hold" from "how to safely get there" from "how to place the order" into four separately-testable stages. Indicators register either manually (`indicator.Update()`) or automatically (`RegisterIndicator` wired to a consolidator) — docs explicitly warn against double-updating by mixing both modes for the same indicator.

**ORDER MODEL**: Market, Limit, StopMarket, StopLimit, TrailingStop (fills as market once triggered), MarketOnOpen, MarketOnClose, LimitIfTouched, and genuine **multi-leg combo orders** (`ComboMarketOrder`/`ComboLimitOrder`/`ComboLegLimitOrder`, each leg able to carry its own limit) — relevant for options/futures spread strategies. **No native OCO** — must be emulated via manual `OnOrderEvent` handling. Every placement returns an `OrderTicket`; fills/status changes surface as `OrderEvent`s to `OnOrderEvent`.

**EXECUTION MODEL**: The strongest, most directly-relevant precedent found across all seven platforms — LEAN's `Security` composes **four independently pluggable interfaces**: `IFillModel`, `ISlippageModel`, `IFeeModel`, `IBuyingPowerModel`/`ISecurityMarginModel` — each separately overridable by subclassing, so fills/slippage/fees/margin are four orthogonal, composable concerns rather than one broker black box. `IBrokerageModel` implementations (e.g. `InteractiveBrokersBrokerageModel`) bundle a DEFAULT combination of these four to emulate a specific real broker's constraints for both backtest realism and live-order validation. **Verified directly from `EquityFillModel.cs` source**: there is no single global "high hits before low" assumption — each order type has its own bar-range test with documented reasoning: `StopMarketFill` triggers off High/Low, then checks for an "unfavorable gap" (fills at Open if the gap is through the stop, else at stop price ± slippage); `LimitFill` requires the bar's Low/High to have STRICTLY passed the limit (not merely equalled it — source comment explicitly states equalling the limit doesn't guarantee the order was at the front of the book queue at that price) and fills at Open on a "favorable gap"; `StopLimitFill` deliberately uses the bar's Close (not a second extreme-value test) for its post-trigger limit check, with a source comment explaining this avoids assuming a favorable intrabar sequencing the OHLC data cannot actually prove. This is a directly citable precedent for AT24's own Intrabar Ambiguity Engine (`Q0.4_INTRABAR_ENGINE.md`) — LEAN's authors consciously chose conservative, DOCUMENTED tie-breaking rules specifically because OHLC cannot prove intrabar sequencing, matching AT24's own "never silently favorable" principle almost exactly. A cited GitHub issue (#963) notes the limit-fill logic can still be "very optimistic" versus real queue position — an honest, acknowledged limitation even in a well-designed system.

**POSITION MODEL**: `SecurityHolding` tracks `Quantity`/`AveragePrice`/`Invested`/`UnrealizedProfit`, with realized P&L booked incrementally per fill via `Portfolio.ProcessFill`. Default cost-basis method is **weighted-average**, not FIFO lot tracking (docs explicitly note this produces different realized-cost numbers than FIFO would).

**ACCOUNT MODEL**: `Portfolio` (a `SecurityPortfolioManager`) aggregates `Invested`/`TotalUnrealizedProfit`/`TotalPortfolioValue`/`TotalMarginUsed`. `IBuyingPowerModel` determines order affordability and effective leverage — `CashBuyingPowerModel` for cash accounts (no leverage), brokerage-credit-aware variants for margin accounts, asset-specific variants for futures/options/pattern-day-trading rules. LEAN does **not** auto-detect account type from a live connection — it must be explicitly configured, a deliberate anti-silent-assumption design choice worth adopting. **Settlement delay is explicitly modeled** via `ISettlementModel`: `ImmediateSettlementModel` (default for most) vs. `DelayedSettlementModel` (buy fills debit cash immediately; sell fills route proceeds to an "unsettled cash" bucket, usable only after the settlement period elapses) — used specifically for US equities/options in cash (non-margin) accounts, mirroring real T+settlement rules, including a documented T+2→T+1 transition consistent with the industry-wide May 2024 US settlement change.

**TIME MODEL**: A precise, verified **three-way** timezone split — Algorithm timezone (the single reference clock, default `America/New_York`, changeable via `SetTimeZone()`), Exchange timezone (per-`Security`, reflecting the actual listing exchange regardless of algorithm timezone), Data timezone (whatever the raw vendor data is stamped in, which may differ from both). LEAN converts data→exchange→algorithm timezone automatically and synchronizes multi-exchange-timezone securities into one algorithm-timezone-referenced `Slice` stream. Market hours/holidays are per-exchange via a `MarketHoursDatabase`.

**BACKTEST MODEL**: `AlgorithmManager` ("the heartbeat of LEAN," per its own docs/community wiki) drives a single unified event clock: pulls all active subscriptions, synchronizes multi-resolution/multi-security data into one time-ordered `Slice` per clock tick, dispatches to `OnData`, processes pending fills for that timestamp, fires due Scheduled Events (with gap-catch-up), advances — this single-clock-for-everything design is exactly what gives LEAN determinism across arbitrarily many securities/resolutions, a direct precedent for `Q0.4_EVENT_MODEL.md`'s `(timestamp, sequence)` total-order requirement. Alpha Streams (a strategy marketplace) is **confirmed discontinued** — QuantConnect's own stated reasoning cited overfitting risk in the vetting process and a scaling mismatch in the one-alpha-per-server architecture; treat as legacy, not current. Cloud Optimization exists (parallelized parameter sweep). Walk-forward is a **documented pattern** built from Scheduled Events + Optimization primitives, not a packaged one-click feature (a GitHub issue and forum threads asking for native WFO suggest this gap is real and known). Monte Carlo is possible in the Jupyter Research environment; QuantConnect's own docs characterize it as "very seldom used for this purpose, since you would be under heavy assumptions" — an unusually honest platform-vendor caveat about a feature's own limitations, worth noting as a tone precedent.

## Backtrader (Python)

**DATA MODEL**: `bt.feeds` provides OHLCV feed classes into `Cerebro` via `adddata()`. Two distinct, precisely-verified aggregation mechanisms: `resampledata()` buffers lower-timeframe bars and emits the larger bar only once COMPLETE (no intrabar visibility); `replaydata()` also aggregates toward a larger target but re-delivers the IN-PROGRESS higher-timeframe bar repeatedly as each new lower-timeframe bar arrives, so `next()` fires multiple times per forming target bar — a genuine, working precedent for AT24's Bar Magnification concept (`Q0.4_INTRABAR_ENGINE.md`), already implemented and shipping in a real, widely-used library. Multiple feeds are wall-clock-synchronized by Cerebro so `next()` sees all feeds' current bars together.

**STRATEGY MODEL**: `next()` is called once per already-CLOSED bar per data feed; `Cerebro` is the top-level orchestrator (data feeds, broker, strategies, sizers, analyzers, observers). Indicators use a lazy "lines" architecture that auto-recomputes per new bar with no manual update call needed (contrast with LEAN, which needs either manual `.Update()` or `RegisterIndicator` wiring). Strategy state persists naturally as instance attributes, since Cerebro reuses the same persistent instance across bars rather than reconstructing it.

**ORDER MODEL**: Market, Close, Limit, Stop (triggers then market-fills), StopLimit (triggers then limit-fills at a separate `pricelimit`), StopTrail (absolute or percent trailing distance, moves only favorably), StopTrailLimit (trailing that fills as a limit once triggered). Bracket/OCO orders exist via `buy_bracket()`/`sell_bracket()` and an explicit OCO linkage mechanism.

**EXECUTION MODEL**: The clearest, most explicitly-labeled lookahead-avoidance precedent found in this entire research pass. **Default next-bar-Open fill, verified precisely**: because `next()` only ever sees an already-closed bar, an order placed inside it cannot be matched against that same bar — the earliest possible fill is the FOLLOWING bar, at its Open, by default (Backtrader's own docs frame this as an explicit "x / x+1" model). **`cheat_on_open` (`cerebro.broker.set_coo(True)`)** deliberately opts INTO same-bar-open visibility via an extra `next_open`-style cycle that runs before order evaluation — docs and even the feature's own name ("cheat") are explicit that this is a conscious lookahead trade-off, not a hidden default; a companion `cheat_on_close` exists for same-bar-close fills (mechanics confirmed to exist, some fine detail sourced from community rather than core docs). This "cheat", named as such, is the single strongest real-world precedent for AT24's own "never silently select the favorable result" principle (`Q0.4_INTRABAR_ENGINE.md`) — Backtrader's maintainers chose to make the unsafe option UNMISSABLY labeled rather than quietly available. Slippage via `slip_perc`/`slip_fixed` broker parameters (existence confirmed; exact current default values not independently re-verified against the live doc page). Commission via `CommInfoBase`, supporting percentage or fixed schemes with an asset multiplier, extensible by subclassing for anything beyond the simple case.

**POSITION MODEL**: `Position` tracks `size`/`price` per data feed (`self.getposition(data)` for feeds beyond `datas[0]`). **No native hedging is strongly inferred** (not found as an explicit doc statement, but the `Position` API's one-object-per-feed shape is only consistent with net-position-per-instrument tracking, not simultaneous independent long+short holdings) — flagged as inferred rather than directly documented.

**ACCOUNT MODEL**: `BackBroker` simulates cash (default starting balance 10,000), automatic per-fill AND per-bar cash adjustment (the latter relevant for mark-to-market instruments like futures, where a price move alone implies a cash gain/loss even without a new trade), order validation against available margin (`checksubmit=True` by default), commission via the `CommInfoBase` objects above.

**TIME MODEL**: Internally **UTC-first** — a materially different design from LEAN's exchange-aware three-way split. Non-UTC input is converted on ingestion via a per-feed `tzinput` parameter (a `pytz`-compatible object); a separate per-feed `tz` parameter controls the DISPLAY timezone shown to the strategy (so a trader can see US-listed bars in `US/Eastern` regardless of their own location); Timers accept a `tzdata` parameter (`None`/a `pytz` instance/inherit-from-a-feed). No built-in per-exchange market-hours/holiday calendar database was found in this research pass (contrast with LEAN's `MarketHoursDatabase`).

**BACKTEST MODEL**: Cerebro's loop, per iteration: deliver notifications for orders resolved on the PRIOR step → evaluate/execute pending orders against the newly-arrived bar → run `next()` against that same new bar (which may issue new orders, matchable only from the FOLLOWING iteration per the x+1 rule) — this precise ordering is what mechanically enforces the no-lookahead default. `optstrategy()` runs the Cartesian product of parameter ranges (optionally multiprocessed) — genuinely native. Walk-forward is **confirmed NOT native** — achieved only via manually driving `optstrategy` over successive rolling windows in user code. Monte Carlo is **confirmed NOT native** — would need external bootstrap-resampling code against an Analyzer's output. Built-in `Analyzers` (SharpeRatio, DrawDown, TradeAnalyzer) cover basic stats; richer reporting commonly routes through the external `pyfolio` (via a built-in `PyFolio` analyzer bridge) or `quantstats` libraries, not a Backtrader-native analytics engine.

## NinjaTrader (NinjaScript, NT8)

**DATA MODEL**: Natively supports a broad set of bar-construction methods — time-based, Tick, Volume, Range, Renko, Kagi, Point & Figure, Line Break, Heiken Ashi, plus order-flow-specific bars (Volumetric, Delta, Price-on-Volume) — documented at NinjaTrader's official Bar Types page. Notably, NinjaTrader's OWN documentation admits a backtesting-fidelity caveat for the price-driven types: backtesting Renko/P&F "can yield different results than what you would experience in real-time, due to the nature of how the bars are constructed and the possibility of not having enough granular information" — a first-party admission matching Pine's stricter warning (below), though NinjaTrader treats these as legitimate native chart types rather than discouraging their use outright. Tick data quality/coverage is provider-dependent (bundled Kinetick feed vs. premium IQFeed/CQG/Rithmic); tick timestamps are normalized to millisecond granularity even where a provider supplies finer resolution. Core bars are Last-trade-based (OHLCVT); bid/ask and Level 2 data are accessible via separate mechanisms, not the default series.

**STRATEGY MODEL**: The `Calculate` property (`OnBarClose`/`OnEachTick`/`OnPriceChange`) is the core recalculation-timing mechanism — precisely matching AT24's own `Q0.4_EVENT_MODEL.md` taxonomy. **Critical, verified fact**: regardless of `Calculate` setting, historical backtests process at bar-close only — "only the OHLCVT of the bar is known [historically] and not each tick that made up the bar." `OnEachTick`/`OnPriceChange` only actually change behavior in REALTIME, not standard backtests — true intrabar historical granularity requires enabling TickReplay or a secondary finer series. This is the single cleanest documented confirmation, across all seven researched platforms, of exactly the historical-vs-realtime asymmetry `Q0.4_LOOKAHEAD_REPAINTING.md`/`Q0.4_STRATEGY_IR.md` treat as a first-class design concern.

**ORDER MODEL**: Two parallel architectures — Managed Approach (`EnterLong`/`SetStopLoss`/`SetProfitTarget`/`SetTrailStop`, native OCO bookkeeping, convenient but a `Set*` call can't be cancelled directly since it returns no `Order` object) vs. Unmanaged Approach (`SubmitOrderUnmanaged`/`ChangeOrder`/`CancelOrder`, full manual control, no guardrails). Order types: Market, Limit, Stop, Stop-Limit, MIT (Market-If-Touched).

**EXECUTION MODEL**: The most consequential, best-documented area. Two fill algorithms: "Default" (price must PENETRATE a limit to fill — conservative) and "Liberal" (fills on mere touch), with a separate "Fill Limit Orders On Touch" checkbox. **Base-case fill resolution, quoted directly from NinjaTrader's own docs**: "Fills are determined based on 4 data points, OHLC of a bar since that is the only information that is known during a backtest and there will be no intra-bar data" — i.e. exactly the same OHLC-ambiguity problem `Q0.4_INTRABAR_ENGINE.md` treats as mandatory. Two DISTINCT, MUTUALLY EXCLUSIVE mitigation mechanisms exist for two DIFFERENT problems: "High(er) Order Fill Resolution" (a secondary, finer series purely for fill-price resolution, 5-20x slower, incompatible with Tick Replay) improves fill ACCURACY; "Tick Replay" (reconstructs synthetic tick events for intrabar STRATEGY CALCULATION) explicitly does NOT improve fill accuracy — NinjaTrader's own docs state "Tick Replay was not designed for order fill accuracy in backtests," since the Strategy Analyzer still resolves fills from bar OHLC even with it enabled. This is a valuable, citable precedent that "resolving when the strategy recalculates" and "resolving what price an order fills at" are genuinely separate problems requiring separate mechanisms — directly validating `Q0.4_EVENT_MODEL.md`'s decision to keep Calculation Timing (Q0.4.6) and the Intrabar/Fill engine (`Q0.4_INTRABAR_ENGINE.md`) as architecturally distinct concerns rather than one setting. Slippage/commission default to **zero** — third-party guides cited note this can overstate profit factor by 30-50% versus realistic costs, directly matching `Q0.4_BACKTEST_FAILURE_CATALOG.md` items 17/18.

**POSITION MODEL**: **Netting only, confirmed directly by a NinjaTrader representative** — "hedging accounts will not be supported in NinjaTrader 8" (tracked as an unresolved feature request). Pyramiding via `EntriesPerDirection` (max concurrent same-direction entries) + `EntryHandling` (`AllEntries` pooled cap vs. `UniqueEntries` per-signal-name cap) — a clean, native mechanism worth comparing against AT24's own not-yet-built pyramiding design.

**ACCOUNT MODEL**: Configurable starting balance/commission/slippage; backtest-time margin/buying-power engine mechanics were not found documented in sufficient depth to cite with confidence (flagged unverified by the research pass).

**TIME MODEL**: Session Templates define per-instrument trading-hours windows. A documented footgun: changing the platform's timezone DISPLAY setting does NOT retroactively shift times already entered in a Trading Hours template — those remain interpreted in whatever zone was active at entry time. Directly relevant precedent for `Q0.4_BACKTEST_FAILURE_CATALOG.md` item 9 (timezone mismatch) — this is exactly the kind of silent, display-vs-stored-value confusion AT24's `Intl.DateTimeFormat`-explicit-zone discipline (Q0.3, extended in `Q0.4_BACKTEST_ARCHITECTURE.md`'s Session/Calendar Engine) is designed to avoid entirely.

**BACKTEST MODEL**: Strategy Analyzer runs against any chosen series/date range. Two optimizer algorithms (Default/exhaustive, Genetic). **Walk-forward optimization is natively supported** (not an add-on) — configurable in-sample "Optimization period" and out-of-sample "Test period" (days), automatically rolled forward, reporting each out-of-sample segment plus its winning parameters in a results grid — the strongest native-WFO precedent found across all seven platforms (contrast with MT4/MT5/LEAN/Backtrader, none of which have this natively). Monte Carlo is listed in NinjaTrader's own documentation navigation as a Strategy Analyzer capability area, though its full mechanics were not independently confirmed in this research pass (flagged partially verified). Playback/Market Replay (a separate, non-Strategy-Analyzer connection type) replays full tick+Level-2 data and is regarded, including by NinjaTrader's own forum staff, as more realistic than standard OHLC-fill backtesting — but it is a manual/semi-live tool, not used for programmatic optimization sweeps.

## cTrader (cAlgo API — C# cBots and native Python)

**DATA MODEL**: Fundamentally **bid/ask-native**, unlike NinjaTrader's Last-trade-centric model — `Symbol.Bid`/`Symbol.Ask` are first-class, always-available properties, reflecting cTrader's ECN/ FX-liquidity-provider origins. Tick data quality is **broker-dependent, not a uniform platform guarantee** — cTrader's own docs and community forum both note some brokers supply real recorded ticks while others only have M1 bars, with community reports of data anomalies (negative spreads, impossible quotes) on some feeds, particularly demo servers. The commonly-repeated claim that cTrader is "known for high-quality tick data" should be treated as broker-dependent, not platform-universal.

**STRATEGY MODEL**: `OnStart()`/`OnTick()`/`OnBar()`/`OnStop()` lifecycle; both `OnTick()` and `OnBar()` can coexist in one cBot. A documented, important interaction: "if you want to backtest a cBot with logic in `OnTick()`, then you should be using tick data" — using tick-driven logic against M1-bar-generated synthetic ticks will misrepresent real intrabar behavior, a direct precedent for `Q0.4_INTRABAR_ENGINE.md`'s Data Fidelity tiering. **Native Python support (added v5.4, ~August 2025)** runs on the SAME underlying `cAlgo.API`/execution engine via .NET/CLR interop, not a separate reimplementation — `on_start()`/`on_tick()`/`on_bar_closed()` mirror the C# events exactly, with cTrader's own docs stating it "manages concurrency by serializing your event-based callbacks... never in parallel." This is a useful, concrete precedent for AT24's own future multi-language IR-translation ambitions (`Q0.4_STRATEGY_IR.md`): cTrader chose ONE execution engine with TWO language front-ends, rather than two parallel engines that could semantically drift apart.

**ORDER MODEL**: Explicit Position (immediate, via `ExecuteMarketOrder`) vs. PendingOrder (`PlaceLimitOrder`/`PlaceStopOrder`, becomes a Position on trigger) distinction. Market/Limit/Stop/Stop-Limit all supported at the platform level, though `PlaceStopLimitOrder` was notably ABSENT from early cAlgo API versions and only added in API 3.0 — a concrete, citable example of API-vs-UI capability lag (the UI could place stop-limit orders before automation could) worth flagging as a design lesson: AT24's own `StrategySpec`/order-type surface should not assume parity between what a platform's UI supports and what its automation API exposes when reasoning about Source Fidelity (Q0.4.34) for a translated strategy.

**EXECUTION MODEL**: The popular claim "cTrader backtests tick-by-tick by default, unlike MT4/5" is **NOT fully confirmed** by official docs — cTrader's own backtesting guide frames tick-vs-bar as a user-selected tradeoff, describing M1-bar-derived synthetic tick generation as "a good compromise between accuracy and the resource-intensiveness of backtesting," not stating tick-by-tick as the unconditional default. Spread modeling depends on this same choice: bar-data backtests use Fixed or Random spread (with a "snap to live" helper); tick-data backtests embed the REAL historical spread per-tick directly — this is the one place cTrader's "genuinely realistic" reputation is directly confirmed, conditional on tick data being used. **No explicit first-party slippage parameter was found** in the backtesting settings docs — a real, documented gap relative to NinjaTrader's explicit slippage-in-ticks setting, worth flagging in `Q0.4_ADOPT_IMPROVE_AVOID_INVENT.md`. Commission is rate-per-million-units-traded (matching real FX ECN billing structure).

**POSITION MODEL**: **Both hedging and netting are supported today** (official docs), with netting accounts using FIFO to determine which lots close first on partial offsets. The historical claim that cTrader was netting-only before later adding hedging is only PARTIALLY confirmed — a 2016/17 Spotware press release documents hedging support added specifically to the FIX API (institutional integration path), not necessarily dated for the core retail desktop platform; treat "netting-only-then-hedging-added" as plausible but not fully verified. Broker-side support for either mode is not guaranteed even where the platform supports both.

**ACCOUNT MODEL**: Margin = position volume ÷ leverage; supports dynamic/tiered leverage. A documented, real approximation gap: `Position.GetEstimatedMargin()` explicitly does NOT account for existing account exposure during backtesting/optimization — margin-aware position sizing logic cannot fully trust the backtest-time estimate, a concrete, citable example of exactly the "incorrect margin" failure mode (`Q0.4_BACKTEST_FAILURE_CATALOG.md` item 20).

**TIME MODEL**: Not researched to comparable depth as NinjaTrader's — no dedicated official session-template/timezone doc page was located in this pass; flagged as a genuine research gap rather than asserted.

**BACKTEST MODEL**: Exhaustive and Genetic optimizers, both documented (the Genetic one explicitly framed in natural-selection terms: passes=individuals, parameters=genes, fitness=degree of fit). Walk-forward is a documented METHODOLOGY (manually partitioning optimization-result date ranges) rather than a confirmed single-click native mode comparable to NinjaTrader's dedicated Optimization-period/Test-period fields — weaker native support than NinjaTrader's, pending further confirmation. Monte Carlo is **not a built-in feature** — available only via cTrader's plugin/marketplace ecosystem (e.g. third-party add-ons), not first-party.
