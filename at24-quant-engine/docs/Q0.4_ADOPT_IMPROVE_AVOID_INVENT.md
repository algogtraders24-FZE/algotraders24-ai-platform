# Q0.4 Adopt / Improve / Avoid / Invent Report

Q0.4.43, per platform. Full narrative treatment — `Q0.4_PLATFORM_DECISIONS.md` is the compact cross-platform summary table for quick reference; this document is the reasoned "why." Every claim traces back to `Q0.4_PLATFORM_R&D.md`/`Q0.4_PINE_SEMANTICS.md`'s research findings — nothing here is asserted without that grounding.

---

## MT4

**ADOPT**: Nothing structural — MT4's execution model (bar-derived-only tick simulation, ticket-based orders with no order/deal/position separation, no native stop-limit, always-hedging-implicit position model) predates the design clarity later platforms converged on. Its ticket simplicity has some value as a MINIMAL reference case (the simplest possible position model any translator must still support), but nothing worth copying architecturally.

**IMPROVE**: The always-hedging-like ticket model, generalized: a "flat list of independent order tickets, no netting concept" is a legitimate, simple accounting mode worth SUPPORTING (as one configuration of AT24's future account-level Position Accounting Mode, per `Q0.4_BACKTEST_ARCHITECTURE.md`), but AT24 makes it an explicit, named choice rather than the ONLY choice, unlike MT4.

**AVOID**: Bar-only tick simulation (no tick-replay option at all) as a hard architectural ceiling; the fixed-per-tester-session spread with no real bid/ask option; `TimeGMT()` not actually returning true GMT in the tester (a documented, misleading API name); the complete absence of any lookahead/repainting-detection concept — MT4 offers no vocabulary for this failure mode at all.

**INVENT**: Nothing MT4-specific to originate — its main contribution to this research was as the historical BASELINE that MT5, and every later platform, improved on.

## MT5

**ADOPT**: The **Order → Deal → Position** three-way separation is a genuinely good design AT24 should mirror conceptually (Q0's `OrderIntent`/`Position` already gesture at this; MT5's explicit Deal-as-immutable-execution-record concept is worth folding into a future `TradeLedger`, per `Q0.4_BACKTEST_ARCHITECTURE.md`). The documented, PUBLISHED tick-generation algorithm (a specific, non-arbitrary 3-5-3-point distribution scheme, capped at 11 reference points per bar) is a strong precedent for AT24's own Bar Magnification/conservative-heuristic fallback (`Q0.4_INTRABAR_ENGINE.md`'s "Deterministic heuristic" tier) — MT5 shows a documented, defensible heuristic beats an arbitrary one. The three-tier execution-delay model (No Delay / Random with a specific documented distribution / Fixed with measured-ping guidance) is a clean, adoptable shape for AT24's `LatencyModel` (Q0.4.18).

**IMPROVE**: The netting-vs-hedging account mode as a BROKER-set, EEE-immutable-per-account configuration is right in spirit (explicit, not silently assumed) but AT24 should make it explicit at the `StrategySpec`/`RiskSpecification` research-experiment level too, not just account-level, since a research question ("how does this strategy perform under hedging vs. netting semantics?") is exactly the kind of Cross-Fidelity/Cross-Platform comparison `Q0.4_BACKTEST_ARCHITECTURE.md` wants to make routine, which requires the mode to be swappable per-experiment, not fixed per broker account as in MT5. The fixed-per-minute-bar spread in generated-tick mode is improvable — AT24's `SessionDependentSpread`/`VariableSpread` (Q0.4.15) are direct, adopted improvements.

**AVOID**: No native Walk-Forward Optimization or Monte Carlo despite being the more mature/current MetaQuotes platform — a genuine, confirmed gap AT24 should not replicate (Q0.4.37's Statistical Robustness boundary exists specifically so this isn't an afterthought). Depth-of-Market being unavailable in the tester even though it exists live — a silent capability gap between live and backtest that AT24's Execution Reality Grade (Q0.4.36) is designed to make visible rather than silent.

**INVENT**: Nothing MT5-specific to originate beyond what's captured in ADOPT above — MT5's real contribution is proving that a documented, disclosed heuristic (tick generation) is trustworthy where an undisclosed one (MT4's "predefined wave templates") is not.

## TradingView / Pine

Full treatment in `Q0.4_PINE_SEMANTICS.md`; summarized here against the four categories.

**ADOPT**: The `lookahead_off`/`lookahead_on`+`[1]`-offset pattern as the CORRECT default for any future MTF read (AT24 makes the safe pattern the ONLY pattern, not an opt-in choice, per `Q0.4_BACKTEST_ARCHITECTURE.md`'s MTF Engine — an improvement over Pine's own "recommend but don't enforce" stance). The explicit, repeated warning against backtesting on synthetic chart types (Heikin Ashi/Renko/etc.) — directly adopted as AT24's `REAL_MARKET_PRICE`/`SYNTHETIC_PRICE` classification (`Q0.4_INTRABAR_ENGINE.md`), generalized beyond Pine's narrower Heikin-Ashi-only mitigation. The margin-call "liquidate 4x to avoid event-thrash" idea, as an option (not mandatory) for a future `MarginModel`. The historical-vs-realtime asymmetry as a NAMED, first-class concept (Pine's clearest single contribution) — directly adopted into `Q0.4_STRATEGY_IR.md`'s Recalculation Semantics field and `Q0.4_LOOKAHEAD_REPAINTING.md`'s repainting-cause catalog.

**IMPROVE**: The default limit-order fill rule (fills on mere touch, not proven trade-through) is exactly backwards from AT24's stance — Pine's OWN stricter opt-in mode (`backtest_fill_limits_assumption`) is the right BEHAVIOR, but AT24 makes it the DEFAULT, never opt-in (per `Q0.4_BACKTEST_FAILURE_CATALOG.md` item 15). Bar Magnifier's 200,000-bar cap silently producing WITHIN-ONE-RUN fidelity inconsistency (early bars unmagnified, later bars magnified) is improved by making AT24's `DataFidelity` a per-result-or-per-trade field, never an assumed-constant-for-the-whole-run property.

**AVOID**: Repainting as a widespread, structurally-possible outcome of ordinary, commonly-used flags (`calc_on_every_tick`, unlabeled `request.security()` misuse) rather than something requiring deliberate, hard-to-reach misuse — Pine's execution model makes several major footguns reachable via single boolean flags with non-obvious consequences; AT24's design goal is that the SAFE choice requires no flag at all (it's simply how the engine works), and the unsafe choice, if it ever exists, is as unmissably labeled as Backtrader's `cheat_on_*`.

**INVENT**: Deep Backtesting's data-windowing-affects-signals discovery (two runs over "the same" nominal range differing purely by calculation-warmup start point) is the clearest real-world justification found in this entire research pass for `Q0.4_BACKTEST_ARCHITECTURE.md`'s strict Provenance requirement — AT24 should treat this as a design-validating case study, not just a Pine quirk to note.

## NinjaTrader 8

**ADOPT**: **Native Walk-Forward Optimization with a dedicated, automated rolling in-sample/out-of-sample UI** — the single strongest native-WFO precedent across all seven platforms; directly informs what a future AT24 Robustness system's WFO mode should look like operationally (`Q0.4_BACKTEST_ARCHITECTURE.md`'s Statistical Robustness Boundary, Q0.4.37). The explicit separation between "High(er) Order Fill Resolution" (improves FILL accuracy) and "Tick Replay" (improves CALCULATION accuracy, explicitly NOT fill accuracy, per NinjaTrader's own docs) is a strong, citable validation of AT24's own decision to keep Calculation Timing (Q0.4.6) and the Intrabar/Fill engine (`Q0.4_INTRABAR_ENGINE.md`) as separate architectural concerns.

**IMPROVE**: The "Default (penetrate) vs. Liberal (touch)" fill-algorithm choice is the right SHAPE (an explicit, named choice) but the wrong DEFAULT direction if "Liberal" is ever the out-of-box setting — AT24 defaults to the conservative/penetrate behavior always, per the sprint's "never silently favorable" mandate. `EntriesPerDirection`/`EntryHandling` (pyramiding) is a clean, adoptable shape, improved by AT24 tying it explicitly to `RiskSpecification.maxSimultaneousPositions` (Q0.3) rather than being a separate, disconnected strategy property.

**AVOID**: Zero-by-default slippage/commission (third-party sources cite 30-50% profit-factor overstatement risk) — directly the failure mode Q0.2's `costsExplicitlyZero` guard already prevents; AT24 must never regress to a silent-zero default anywhere in the pipeline. The timezone-display-vs-stored-value footgun (changing display timezone doesn't retroactively shift already-entered session-template times) — AT24's `Intl.DateTimeFormat`-explicit-zone discipline (Q0.3, extended in `Q0.4_BACKTEST_ARCHITECTURE.md`) structurally avoids this class of bug by never separating "stored" from "displayed" timezone at all.

**INVENT**: Nothing NinjaTrader-specific to originate — its contribution is validating two existing AT24 design decisions (separate calc-timing from fill-resolution; native WFO as a real, buildable capability) rather than suggesting a wholly new one.

## cTrader

**ADOPT**: The bid/ask-native data model (as opposed to Last-trade-centric) as AT24's PREFERRED representation wherever D5 fidelity is available — matches AT24's own `Q0.4_INTRABAR_ENGINE.md` fidelity ladder's top tiers directly. The Position-vs-PendingOrder API distinction is clean and matches AT24's own Order Lifecycle design (`Q0.4_EVENT_MODEL.md`) closely. Supporting BOTH netting and hedging natively, as a real, currently-shipping platform capability (not just a documented-but-unconfirmed idea), reinforces that AT24's own dual-mode Position Accounting design (informed by MT5's research) is a proven, buildable choice, not speculative.

**IMPROVE**: The single-engine-two-language-frontends design (native Python and C# cBots sharing the identical `cAlgo.API`/execution engine, not two parallel reimplementations) is directly adoptable as a PRINCIPLE for AT24's own future multi-source-language ambitions (`Q0.4_STRATEGY_IR.md`) — AT24 should ensure any future MQL/Pine/NinjaScript-sourced strategy translation targets the SAME `StrategySpec`/runtime, never a parallel execution path per source language, exactly as cTrader avoided a Python-vs-C# semantic split.

**AVOID**: The documented API-vs-UI capability lag (stop-limit orders available in the UI before the automation API supported them) — AT24 should never let its own `StrategySpec`/order-type surface silently lag behind what a future UI/product layer promises; Source Fidelity grading (Q0.4.34) exists partly to catch exactly this class of mismatch when translating FROM a platform where it occurred. The absence of a documented native slippage parameter, and `GetEstimatedMargin()`'s documented failure to account for existing exposure during backtesting — both concrete, citable instances of the "hidden execution assumption"/"incorrect margin" failure modes (`Q0.4_BACKTEST_FAILURE_CATALOG.md` items 18/20) AT24 exists to structurally prevent.

**INVENT**: Nothing cTrader-specific to originate — its main contribution is the single-engine-multi-frontend validation above.

## QuantConnect / LEAN

**ADOPT — the strongest single precedent found in all of Q0.4's research.** The four-interface execution separation (`IFillModel`/`ISlippageModel`/`IFeeModel`/`IBuyingPowerModel`, independently overridable, composed by `IBrokerageModel`) is adopted almost directly as `Q0.4_BACKTEST_ARCHITECTURE.md`'s Execution Reality models section — AT24's `FeeModel`/`SpreadModel`/`SlippageModel`/`FillModel`/`MarginModel`/`LatencyModel` (already reserved as interfaces in Q0.2's `reality-models.ts`) are structurally the same idea, independently arrived at before this research confirmed LEAN got there first and shipped it in production. `EquityFillModel.cs`'s per-order-type, documented, conservative OHLC-range tie-breaking rules (no assumed global "high before low," a Close-based confirmation for stop-limit orders specifically BECAUSE the source authors reasoned OHLC can't prove intrabar sequencing) is the single most directly-citable, code-verified validation of AT24's entire Intrabar Ambiguity Engine (`Q0.4_INTRABAR_ENGINE.md`) — this is real, shipped code reaching the same conclusion AT24's design does, independently. The explicit three-way Algorithm/Exchange/Data timezone split is adopted as the model for a future Session/Calendar Engine (Q0.4.29) needing per-instrument accuracy beyond Q0.3's single-timezone session gating. The `ISettlementModel` (Immediate vs. Delayed, explicitly modeling T+settlement for cash accounts) is adopted directly as the shape for AT24's reserved `SettlementModel` (Q0.4.20). The Algorithm Framework's Alpha/Portfolio-Construction/Risk/Execution module separation independently validates AT24's own Decision → Risk Evaluation → Order Engine pipeline separation (Q0.3, `Q0.4_BACKTEST_ARCHITECTURE.md`) — four platforms' worth of convergent design (LEAN's framework, cTrader's Position/PendingOrder split, MT5's Order/Deal/Position split, and AT24's own Q0.3 Risk/Execution boundary) all independently arrive at "separate the DECISION from the ACT."

**IMPROVE**: LEAN not auto-detecting real account type/leverage from a connected brokerage (requiring explicit `AccountType.Cash` configuration) is adopted as a PRINCIPLE — but AT24 goes further, requiring EVERY execution assumption to be explicit (Q0.2's `costsExplicitlyZero` guard already does this for costs; the same discipline should extend to margin/settlement assumptions once those models are built).

**AVOID**: Walk-forward as a "documented recipe" (Scheduled Events + Optimization primitives) rather than a packaged capability, and Monte Carlo explicitly characterized by QuantConnect's own docs as "seldom used... under heavy assumptions" — LEAN, despite being the most architecturally mature platform researched, still leaves Statistical Robustness (Q0.4.37) as a build-it-yourself exercise; AT24 should not consider its own job done just because the Execution Reality layer is well-designed — Robustness needs equal design attention in a future sprint, not an afterthought the way it apparently remained even for LEAN.

**INVENT**: Nothing LEAN-specific to originate beyond adopting its proven patterns — its contribution IS the adoption list above.

## Backtrader

**ADOPT**: The default next-bar-Open fill as the SAFE baseline (an order placed inside `next()`, which only ever sees an already-closed bar, cannot be matched against that same bar — the earliest possible fill is the FOLLOWING bar's open) is adopted directly as `Q0.4_EVENT_MODEL.md`'s `NEXT_BAR_OPEN` default recommendation. The **`cheat_on_open`/`cheat_on_close` naming itself** is the strongest single precedent found in this entire research pass for how to label an unsafe-but-sometimes-legitimate lookahead-permitting mode: Backtrader's own maintainers named the feature "cheat," making the trade-off unmissable rather than hiding it behind an innocuous-sounding flag (contrast with Pine's `process_orders_on_close`, whose name gives no hint it introduces same-bar-fill risk). AT24 adopts this naming DISCIPLINE explicitly (`Q0.4_INTRABAR_ENGINE.md`'s "never silently select the favorable result" principle) — any future AT24 mode that permits intentional lookahead for research purposes must be named with the same bluntness Backtrader used, not a neutral-sounding technical term.

**IMPROVE**: `replaydata()`'s live re-delivery of an in-progress higher-timeframe bar (built from lower-timeframe bars arriving one at a time) is a working, shipping precedent for AT24's `BarDetailProvider`/Bar Magnification concept (`Q0.4_INTRABAR_ENGINE.md`) — AT24 improves on it by making the Data Fidelity level (D2/D3, Q0.4.12) an explicit, queryable property of the result, rather than an implicit consequence of which Cerebro method was called.

**AVOID**: No native Walk-Forward or Monte Carlo (confirmed, matching MT4/MT5/cTrader) — the third of four Python/general-purpose-adjacent platforms researched (Backtrader, LEAN's Research-environment Monte Carlo, cTrader's plugin-based approach) to leave this as an external/manual exercise; strengthens the case that Statistical Robustness (Q0.4.37) genuinely needs first-class AT24 attention rather than assuming any single researched platform already solved it well enough to copy.

**INVENT**: Nothing Backtrader-specific to originate beyond the naming-discipline adoption above — its contribution, like LEAN's, is validating rather than suggesting.

## Cross-cutting theme (all seven platforms)

The single most consistent finding across this entire research pass: **every platform that has thought carefully about intrabar/lookahead ambiguity (LEAN's fill model source comments, Pine's documented historical/realtime asymmetry, Backtrader's honestly-named cheat flags, NinjaTrader's own admission about Renko/P&F fidelity) arrives at conservative, disclosed, opt-in-for-unsafe designs — and every platform that hasn't (MT4's undisclosed "predefined wave templates," Pine's lenient-by-default limit fills, cTrader's undocumented slippage handling) leaves a real, citable gap.** This is the strongest possible evidentiary basis for `Q0.4_INTRABAR_ENGINE.md`'s core mandate: AT24 is not inventing caution where none existed elsewhere — it is choosing, deliberately, to sit on the disclosed-and-conservative side of a line every mature platform researched here has, in some form, already had to draw.
